Import

Five ways to get a QR code in.

Google Authenticator's "Transfer accounts" codes (all accounts or a selection, over several codes) and any service's own otpauth:// code.

Viewfinder

A see-through window you drag over a code anywhere on screen. Only what is inside its frame is read.

⇧⌘F

Whole screen or a region

Read every code on your displays (a mirrored phone, a video call), or draw a rectangle around one.

⇧⌘S ⇧⌘R

Camera

The built-in camera, a USB webcam, or your iPhone as Continuity Camera. Stays open for multi-code transfers.

⇧⌘K

Image, paste or drop

Open a screenshot, paste one from the clipboard, or drop it onto the window.

⌘O ⇧⌘V

Setup key

Type the key a service shows next to its QR code. TOTP or HOTP, SHA-1/256/512, 6–8 digits, any period.

⌘N

Re-scan without worry

A banner tracks "Received 1 of 3 QR codes". Accounts you already have are skipped, even after you renamed them.

Two minutes

From your phone to your Mac.

Your phone keeps its accounts: exporting only shows them.

  1. In Google Authenticator on the phone: ☰ → Transfer accounts → Export accounts, and pick all accounts or a few.
  2. On the Mac, in rAuthenticator: + → Scan with Camera… and hold the phone up, or mirror it and use the viewfinder.
  3. Keep scanning until the banner says all codes are in. From then on, switch to the app and just start typing.

Security

Boring, on purpose.

No server, no account, no sync service, no third-party code. Your secrets stay where macOS keeps passwords.

In your Keychain

Secrets live in the login Keychain, never in plain files. The previous list is kept too, so one bad edit can be undone.

A careful clipboard

Copied codes stay on this Mac (no Universal Clipboard), are flagged so clipboard managers skip them, and vanish after 60 seconds.

Export asks first

A setup key is as sensitive as a password, so showing one or exporting everything needs Touch ID or your password.

Hide and lock

Blur the codes for screen sharing; lock with Touch ID on launch, sleep, idle or ⌘L.

Never online

The app makes no network connections at all: no analytics, no update checks, no cloud backup.

Native and small

SwiftUI on macOS 14+, about 2.5 MB to download, tested against the RFC 4226/6238 vectors and Google's transfer format.

Questions

Does my phone lose its accounts?

No. Google Authenticator's export only shows QR codes; the accounts stay on the phone. You end up with the same codes on both.

Is the Mac download safe to open?

Yes. The disk image and the app are signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens them without warnings. Download it only from the GitHub release.

Why does it ask for Screen Recording or Camera?

Only the scanners need them: the viewfinder, screen and region scans read your screen, the camera scan reads the camera. Nothing else uses them, and nothing leaves the Mac.

Can I move accounts between the Mac and the iPhone?

Yes, the same way as from Google Authenticator: Export All on one shows transfer QR codes, the scanner on the other reads them.

Which codes work?

Time-based (TOTP) and counter-based (HOTP), SHA-1, SHA-256 and SHA-512, 6 to 8 digits, any period. Google's transfer format can't carry 7-digit codes or periods other than 30 s; those export one by one instead.

How do I wipe it?

Delete the app's two items in Keychain Access (search for "authenticator"), then the app itself.
